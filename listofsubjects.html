  • GENERAL SCIENCE
  1. LESSON 1: 1st law of motion (law of inertia)
a. Summary: The first law of motion, also known as the law of inertia, states that an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant velocity unless acted upon by an external force.
b. Reflection: This law highlights the natural resistance objects have to changes in their state, emphasizing the importance of external forces in altering motion.
c. Application: Understanding inertia helps in designing safety features like seat belts and in predicting how objects will behave when forces are applied.
  1. LESSON 2: 1st law of motion (law of Acceleration)
a. Summary: The second law of motion states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force applied to it and inversely proportional to its mass, expressed as F = ma
b. Reflection: This law explains how different forces affect objects of varying masses, emphasizing the importance of considering force and mass when analyzing motion.
c. Application: Applying this law helps in calculating the force needed to move or stop objects, which is essential in engineering, vehicle design, and safety analysis.
  1. LESSON 3: 1st law of motion (law of Action-reaction)
a. Summary: The third law of motion states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. This means that forces always come in pairs, exerted by two objects on each other./dd>
b. Reflection: This law highlights the mutual interactions between objects, illustrating that forces are always reciprocal and balanced in magnitude but opposite in direction.
c. Application: The action-reaction principle explains phenomena like why rockets can propel themselves forward by expelling exhaust gases and is fundamental in designing machinery, vehicles, and understanding everyday interactions.
  • GENERAL MATHEMATICS
  1. LESSON 1: Understanding and Computing Salary, Wage, Commission
a. Summary: Salary, wages, and commissions are different forms of income earned by employees. Salary is a fixed regular payment, wages are usually paid based on hours worked, and commission is earnings based on sales or performance.
b. Reflection: Understanding these income types helps employees and employers manage financial planning, taxation, and motivation strategies effectively.
c. Application: Knowing how to compute salary, wages, and commissions enables accurate payroll processing, budgeting, and incentivizing sales and performance in various professions.
  1. LESSON 2: Computing Holiday and Non-Holiday
a. Summary: Computing holiday and non-holiday pay involves calculating wages based on whether the work occurs on regular days or designated holidays. Typically, holiday pay might be higher due to special rates or allowances.
b. Reflection: Understanding how to compute holiday and non-holiday wages ensures fair compensation and compliance with labor laws, promoting employee satisfaction and legal adherence.
c. Application: Accurate calculation of holiday and non-holiday pay is essential for payroll management, budgeting, and maintaining good employee relations during special workdays.
